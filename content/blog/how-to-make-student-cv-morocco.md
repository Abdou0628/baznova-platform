## Specifics of the Student CV in Morocco <a name="specifics-student-cv-morocco"></a>

The Moroccan student CV shares the general structure of the French-language CV, but it has several **local specifics** that must be mastered.

### 1. The photo is still very common

Unlike France where the photo is increasingly discouraged (risk of discrimination), in Morocco it remains **expected in 70% of cases**, especially for corporate internships and first jobs. But beware: a poorly framed or selfie photo can **eliminate you immediately**.

**Best practices for Moroccan CV photos:**
- Recent professional photo (less than 2 years old)
- Neutral background (white, light gray, or pastel blue)
- Sober outfit (suit-tie or shirt)
- Direct gaze, slight smile
- Square or rectangular format (4.5 × 5.5 cm maximum)

### 2. Mention city and nationality

Moroccan recruiters like to know immediately where you live. Indicate your **city of residence** (Casablanca, Rabat, Fès, Tangier…) at the top of the CV, next to your contact information. Moroccan nationality can be mentioned, especially if you're applying to public or semi-public companies.

### 3. Importance of languages

The Moroccan market is **multilingual by nature**. A good student CV should clearly indicate your level in:
- **Arabic** (mother tongue)
- **French** (main working language)
- **English** (highly valued, especially in tech and finance)
- **Spanish** (asset for Tangier, Tetouan, Northern Morocco)
- **Amazigh** (Tachelhit, Tarifit, or Tamazight) — increasingly valued

Specify your level with the **CEFR framework** (A1 to C2) rather than vague mentions like "read, written, spoken".

### 4. Academic references

In Morocco, the **educational background** remains central for a student. Mention:
- Your institution (university, engineering school, BTS, ISTA…)
- Your program and year of study
- Your baccalaureate with mention and track (Mathematical Sciences, Economic Sciences, Literature…)
- Important grades if they are good (e.g., "General average 16/20")

## Recommended Structure in 2026 <a name="recommended-structure"></a>

Here is the recommended modern structure for a Moroccan student CV on one page:

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────┐
│  Header: Name + Target position      │
│  Contact info + City + LinkedIn      │
├──────────────────────────────────────┤
│  Photo (optional, right side)        │
├──────────────────────────────────────┤
│  Profile / Objective (2-3 lines)     │
├──────────────────────────────────────┤
│  Education (recent → old)            │
├──────────────────────────────────────┤
│  Experience (internships, student    │
│  jobs)                               │
├──────────────────────────────────────┤
│  Skills (technical + soft)           │
├──────────────────────────────────────┤
│  Languages + IT                      │
├──────────────────────────────────────┤
│  Interests (1-2 lines)               │
└──────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Golden rule**: a student CV should **never exceed one page**. Recruiters don't have time to read more.

## The 7 Essential Sections <a name="essential-sections"></a>

### Section 1: Header and Contact Information

Your name should be **the first visible element**, in larger font (18-22 pt). Below, indicate:
- Phone (+212 6 XX XX XX XX format)
- Professional email (firstname.lastname@example.com, no nicknames)
- City of residence
- LinkedIn link (if profile is complete)
- Portfolio/GitHub link (for tech profiles)

### Section 2: CV Title

The CV title should correspond to the **target position**, not your current status. Avoid "Student in Management" and prefer "Management Student - Seeking Digital Marketing Internship". This title also helps ATS categorize you.

### Section 3: Profile / Professional Objective

2 to 3 lines summarizing **who you are and what you're looking for**. Example:

> "3rd-year student at ENCG Casablanca, specializing in finance. Seeking a final-year internship in financial analysis at a major banking group. Bilingual French/English, proficient in Excel and Power BI."

### Section 4: Education

List your degrees **from most recent to oldest**:
- Year obtained (or "in progress")
- Degree title
- Institution + city
- Mention if applicable

Example:
```
2024 - Present: State Engineering Diploma in Computer Science
                ENSIAS, Rabat — Specialization: Software Engineering
                Average: 15.2/20

2021 - 2023: CPGE Preparatory Classes
             Moulay Idriss High School, Meknès — MPSI/MP track
             Admitted ENSIAS competition (rank 124/2400)
```

### Section 5: Professional Experience

Even a student has valuable experiences: internships, summer jobs, volunteering, student projects, freelancing. Structure each experience as follows:

- Period (month-year → month-year)
- Job title / mission
- Organization name + city
- 3 to 5 bullets describing your achievements (with **numbers** if possible)

### Section 6: Skills

Separate:
- **Technical skills** (software, tools, methods)
- **Soft skills** (5 maximum, choose the most relevant)
- **Languages** with CEFR level
- **IT** (Office suite, specific tools, programming languages)

### Section 7: Interests

One to two lines maximum. Prioritize activities that **develop transferable skills**: team sports (teamwork), theater (public speaking), volunteering (commitment), blog (writing).

## What to Include When You Have No Experience? <a name="no-experience"></a>

This is the question every bachelor's degree holder and first-year student asks. Here's what you can highlight instead of professional experience:

### 1. Academic projects

Any substantial school project counts: thesis, case study, group project, hackathon. Describe it as an experience:

> **End-of-semester project — Budget management app**
> ENSA Tangier, March-May 2026
> - Developed a React Native mobile application
> - Teamwork with 4 students (Agile/Scrum method)
> - Presentation to professional jury, grade 17/20

### 2. Volunteering and associational involvement

The Moroccan associational ecosystem is very active (Enactus, AIESEC, university clubs). These commitments show your **proactivity** and sense of responsibility.

### 3. Online certifications

Free or low-cost certifications are an excellent way to demonstrate your skills:
- Google Digital Active (digital marketing)
- HubSpot Academy (inbound marketing, sales)
- Microsoft Learn (Azure, Office, Power Platform)
- Coursera, edX (university courses)
- freeCodeCamp (programming)

### 4. Competitions

National competitions (Olympiads, Hackathons in Morocco, HSE challenge, eloquence competitions…): any podium or participation is worth mentioning.

### 5. Personal work

Blog, YouTube channel, open-source project, thematic Instagram account: any content you created out of passion shows your creativity and motivation.

## Fatal Mistakes to Avoid <a name="fatal-mistakes"></a>

Here are the 10 mistakes that send your CV straight to the trash:

1. **Spelling mistakes**: 1 mistake = direct elimination in 60% of cases. Get proofreading.
2. **CV too long**: a student sending 3 pages looks pretentious.
3. **Ridiculous email addresses**: "leboss2005@hotmail.com" is disqualifying.
4. **Fantasy fonts**: stick to Calibri, Arial, Garamond, or Roboto.
5. **Invisible tables**: Word generates layouts that break on other software.
6. **Unexplained acronyms**: "Bac SM" can be ambiguous outside Morocco.
7. **Useless hobby lists**: "cinema, music, travel" adds nothing.
8. **Lies**: 70% of recruiters verify, especially in France.
9. **CV not adapted to the position**: sending the same CV to 50 companies is counterproductive.
10. **Unreadable PDF format**: some poorly generated PDFs don't pass ATS.

## ATS Optimization for Large Corporations <a name="ats-optimization"></a>

Large Moroccan companies (OCP, Maroc Telecom, BMW, Société Générale Maroc, CIMAR…) use ATS like **Taleo, Workday, or SmartRecruiters**. Here's how to optimize your CV for these automatic filters:

### Rule 1: use the right keywords

Read the internship offer carefully and **reuse the keywords** in your CV (skills, software, methods). If the offer asks for "Power BI", write "Power BI" not "Microsoft BI".

### Rule 2: simple text format

Avoid:
- Headers/footers (often ignored by ATS)
- Complex columns
- Images to convey information
- Word files (.docx) — prefer PDF

### Rule 3: clear hierarchy

Use **standard section titles**: "Education", "Experience", "Skills", "Languages". ATS recognize these patterns.

### Rule 4: full names

Write "Mohammed V University of Rabat" rather than "UM5", "National Superior School of Computer Science and System Analysis" rather than "ENSIAS".

## FAQ <a name="faq"></a>

### Should I mention my baccalaureate even after 3 years of study?

**Yes**, especially in Morocco where the baccalaureate track remains valued. But mention it after your higher education and concisely.

### What length for a student CV?

**One page**, without exception. If you lack space, remove useless interests or irrelevant experiences.

### Should I send the CV in French or Arabic?

Depends on the company. For **large private companies**, French is the norm. For the **public sector and government institutions**, Arabic is mandatory. For **tech startups**, English is increasingly common.

### How to indicate my average if it's not excellent?

If your average is below 12/20, **don't mention it**. Prioritize grades of modules where you excelled, or your projects.

### Can I use a generic CV for all my applications?

No. Adapt at least the **CV title** and **keywords in the skills section** to each offer. ATS detect "generic" CVs and penalize them.
